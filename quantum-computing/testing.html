Quantum Software Testing

Classically, software testing is a well established field which has been extensively researched and employed at scale to produce reliable programs. Modern approaches typically employ testing at a range of levels, from low level unit tests to more complex integration and system tests, as part of automated processes to ensure a program's compliance with a predetermined specification.

Comparatively, quantum software testing is a new field presenting novel challenges and barriers in the creation of reliable software. Perhaps the most impactful changes are as a result of destructive measurement and its rammifications on how testing can be conducted.

Suppose there exists a complex program that we wish to test. One approach may be to take a variable and observe how it is changed throughout the program and checking whether or not its value is within expected bounds.

In the classical case one can easily check the value of the variable and ensure it is with necessary bounds. In the quantum case this is not possible without at least partially collapsing the quantum state the variable is part of. The no cloning theorem also prohibits the copying of a non-trivial quantum variable, preventing one from copying the variable before it is measured.

In this example we have also made the assumption that a program specification is also easily definable. In the quantum case program specification is greatly complicated by the introduction of superposition, which prevents deterministic specification.

These factors combined create a challenging environment for the development of trustworthy quantum software.
