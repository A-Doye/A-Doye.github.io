Quantum Image Processing

Quantum image processing (QIMP) is the study of processing visual information using quantum devices. The field can be split into two main areas:

  • How can we store images in and retrieve images from quantum states?
  • How can we use and manipulate images stored in quantum states?

Long term storage of images on quantum computers is not advisable due to the fragile nature of quantum states. Images stored on current quantum devices quickly become irretrievable due to decoherence, whereas classical devices store images very effectively. Instead of prolonged storage, QIMP focuses on how quantum mechanics may be leveraged to perform some computation on the image more efficiently than classically possible.

QIMP has applied the uniquely quantum properties of superposition and entanglement to research areas including edge detection, machine learning and steganography.

Working with quantum images

Typically, QIMP follows a three step approach:

  1. A classical image is encoded into a quantum state using a parameterised quantum circuit.
  2. The image is manipulated in some way using quantum operations.
  3. Measurements are taken to retrieve the altered image. Usually multiple measurements of the quantum image are needed to construct a complete result.

As multiple measurements of the manipulated state are necessary and quantum states collapse upon measurement, it is necessary to encode and manipulate the image repetedly to gain a complete result. This creates a desire for good encoding algorithms to be efficient and use as few gates as possible when encoding.
