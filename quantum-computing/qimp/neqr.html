Novel Enhanced Quantum Representation (NEQR)

Using the locational format introduced by FRQI, Zhang et. al, 2013 present an alternative method for encoding colour from which it is possible to retrieve an exact copy of the original image.

Instead of utilising superposition to encode colour a qubitstring is utilised. This method dramatically reduce the measurement cycles necessary to retrieve the image at the cost of a modest increase in qubits necessary. A colour qubitstring also enables new manipulations on the encoded image state, as operations may be conditioned upon the value stored within the qubit string.

Encoding colour onto a qubit string

A square, greyscale image with side length n2 and a colour depth of q2 may be stored in the NEQR format using 2n+q qubits. Initial preparation into the intermediary H state is similar to FRQI:

H 02n+q= (I⊗q⊗H⊗2n) 02n+q= 1#2n 0⊗q ⊗ 2n-1~i=0 i= H

The H=I⊗q⊗H⊗2n places the positional qubits in superposition without effecting the colour encoding qubits.

To encode colour the original paper describes a quantum oracle Ωim which transforms the m-th colour encoding qubit to the corresponding classical value of the m-th bit at location i, Cim.

Ωim:0⊗q → 0⊗q-1 ⊗Cim

When reapplied for all values of m at a location, defined as Ωi= ⊗m=0q-1Ωim the oracle has the effect of copying the classical colour bitstring onto the qubit string.

This oracle is useful, as it allows us to directly store classical data in quantum states enabling accurate retrieval. Alone its use would be limited; storing in quantum basis states is equivalent to storing information classically. Quantumness is introduced by entangling colour encoding with the locational qubits, using the same projector process as in FRQI to construct a colour encoding operator Uk

Uk = Ωk ⊗ kk+ I⊗q ⊗ 22n-1~j=0, j≠k jj

Which produces the complete NEQR state when applied for all values of k within the image.

Variations

NEQR and its derivatives are perhaps the most popular encoding format for QIMP currently. The use of a qubit string to enable bitwise logic on the quantum state (through CNOT gates) broadens the format's applicability.

Sang et al, 2017 introduced the Novel Colour representation of Quantum Images (NCQI) format to enable full RGB colour on the NEQR format. This is achieved through the simple addition of additional qubits for each channel, requring 2n+3q qubits. Where fC(i) is the bit string of channel C at position i the NCQI format is expressed:

ΨNCQI= 1#2n 2n-1~i=0 fR(i)fG(i)fB(i) i

The exploration of this approach was continued by Su et. al, 2021 with the addition of a fourth, alpha encoding channel to create the Improved-NCQI format.

An alternative method to include colour channels is presented by Liu et. al, 2018. To enable RGBa colour images 2 additional 'channel' qubits are added to the NEQR format, for a total qubit size of 2n+q+2. These channel qubits are prepared with a Hadamard transform, and the colour encoding operation made additionally dependent upon their state. Ignoring the positional qubits, the channel and colour encoding qubits at location i may take the form:

1#2 (00fR(i)+ 01fG(i)+ 10fB(i)+ 11fa(i))

Li et al, 2018 use a similar approach to reduce the qubits necessary to store an NEQR image. Bitplane Representation of Quantum Images (BRQI) adds a string of bitplane representing qubits, encoding colour onto a single qubit conditioned on the bitplane qubits. The format presented is for greyscale images, though expansion to colour using the techniques described above is trivial.
