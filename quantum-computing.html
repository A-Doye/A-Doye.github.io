Quantum Computing

What's a quantum computer?

Quantum computers use quantum physics to process information in an entirely different way to normal (classical) computers! This allows quantum computers to perform certain processes faster than classical devices. Most famously, Shor's quantum factorisation algorithm can break popular encryption methods such as RSA. Grover's quantum search of unstructured data is also faster than any known classical method.

Implementing a large scale, error corrected quantum device is an ongoing challenge. Quantum bits, or 'qubits' require a physical implementation, with modalities like superconducting and photonic each bringing unique advantages. Qubits are also imperfect and 'noisy', which requires error correcting algorithms to achieve an ideal 'logical' qubits. The no-cloning theorem means that networks of quantum devices will need to be structured and operated diffierently to their classical counterparts.

My interests

I enjoy researching algorithms and developing software related to quantum computers. My undergraduate thesis focused on using quantum computers for processing images. As part of this work I reviewed and compared currently popular algorithms for quantum image processing, as well as the manipulations possible on encoding images. Algorithms analysed were implemented using the qiskit language to run simulations. Most methods of quantum image encoding, such as FRQI rely on highly entangled states to store images with great space efficiency. This reliance makes these algorithms entirely unsuitable for current quantum computing devices for any image at scale.

My masters research explored quantum natural language processing, and the inclusion of images in an existing theoretical framework to produce a multimodal modal. This work utilised the high level quantum programming package Lambeq to classify which of two given sentences related to a given image embedded in a quantum state. Through simulated experiments I was able to demonstrate flaws in existing work, and propose solutions. A limited test case showed that these proposals performed better than previous work.

I continue to be interested in developing practical quantum computing technologies from an algorithmic and software development perspective. Theses available upon request for relevant parties.
